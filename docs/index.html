Abstract

RLHF-aligned language models exhibit response homogenization: on TruthfulQA (n=790), 40–79% of questions produce a single semantic cluster across 10 i.i.d. samples. On affected questions, sampling-based uncertainty methods have zero discriminative power (AUROC=0.500), while free token entropy retains signal (0.603). This alignment tax is task-dependent, caused by DPO (not SFT), recipe-dependent (0.5%–28.5% SCR across families), and generalizes across benchmarks. Stage-wise token entropy measurement reveals the mechanistic decoupling: DPO retains 66% of per-token entropy while collapsing response diversity. Motivated by this diagnosis, we explore a cheapest-first cascade over orthogonal uncertainty signals, achieving 93.2% accuracy on GSM8K at 50% coverage with 57% cost savings.

Key Findings

The Alignment Tax: Aligned models collapse responses into single semantic clusters — 40% (Jaccard) to 79% (embedding) of TruthfulQA questions produce identical answers across 10 samples. Sampling-based UQ has zero signal on these questions.
DPO is the Driver: Training stage ablation on two independent chains (Zephyr, Tulu-3) shows SFT preserves base diversity (≤1.5% SCR) while DPO causes collapse. Severity is recipe-dependent (Zephyr 4.0% vs Tulu-3 0.5%).
Token–Response Decoupling: DPO compresses per-token entropy by only 34% (1.175→0.776 nats) while collapsing response diversity — explaining why B1 token entropy retains discriminative power where sampling fails.
Task-Dependent: Cohen's d = 0.07 on factual QA vs 0.81 on math reasoning — a 10× gap in effect size that demands multi-boundary design.
